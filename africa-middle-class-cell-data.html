The release

The release holds the predicted middle-class shares of 159,698 populated cells of 96 km² in 55 African countries, under six income definitions, with their sums for countries, provinces and districts. The data accompany the paper Mapping Africa’s Middle Class Across Space and are public on Zenodo. They describe the period 2015–2023 as a whole.

File Content
cells.csv.gz, cells.parquet one row per populated cell: identifiers, population and, for each definition, the share, the number of middle-class people and the disagreement of the learners
cells.gpkg the same table with the hexagon of each cell, for GIS software
aggregates.zip population, shares and middle-class numbers by country, by province and by district (first- and second-level administrative units of GADM)
robustness.zip cell shares before the district adjustment, and cell shares from models trained without gridded GDP, the Human Development Index and the two wealth indices, for four definitions
codebook.csv every column of every file, with its unit
README.md, CITATION.cff, LICENSE, SHA256SUMS documentation, citation, license and checksums

Incomes are per capita and per day, in constant 2016 US dollars at the purchasing power parities of the 2017 International Comparison Program, as the Gallup World Poll reports them. Every band includes its lower bound and excludes its upper bound. Banerjee and Duflo (2008) and Ravallion (2010) classify households by consumption, while the Gallup World Poll records income. The classes are therefore an income-based approximation to their concepts.

Method

For each of 829 subnational regions in 45 countries with at least 30 respondents, we compute the survey-weighted share of respondents in each band. We relate these shares to 102 features of the regions: points of interest and networks from the Africa Infrastructure Database (Krantz, 2023), population and its age structure, night lights, travel times, gridded GDP per capita and the Human Development Index (Kummu et al., 2018), and two wealth indices (Lee and Braithwaite, 2022; Chi et al., 2022). A Super Learner combines five learners (gradient boosting, XGBoost, random forests, neural networks and a generalized linear model), with folds that leave out one country at a time. We then evaluate the fitted model at the features of each cell.

Within each district, or province where the data have no districts, one shift on the logit scale makes the population-weighted average of the cell predictions equal the ensemble’s prediction for that district. Population comes from WorldPop (Pezzulo et al., 2017), and from GHS-POP (Schiavina et al., 2023) where WorldPop records none (1,084 cells). The share times the population of a cell gives its number of middle-class people.

Accuracy

In cross-validation that leaves out one country at a time, the R² is 0.72 for the Banerjee and Duflo (2008) band and 0.62 for the Kharas (2010) band, and it lies between 0.49 and 0.72 across the six definitions. This measures accuracy in a country without survey data. For 172 test regions withheld from estimation, the R² lies between 0.65 and 0.88 on the logit scale on which the models are fit. These regions measure accuracy for new regions of surveyed countries. Both compare predictions with regional averages. Within countries, the model explains 46 percent of the variation in regional shares for the $2–10 band and 3 percent for the $10–100 band.

Reading the cells

The cell values are predictions. Every survey share behind them is a regional average, and the models learn how features and middle-class shares move together across regions. The cell maps assume that the same relation holds within regions. The sums for districts, provinces and countries are the firmest numbers in the release, and the district shares equal the ensemble’s predictions for the districts by construction.

Three groups of cells carry a flag. In ten countries (Angola, Cabo Verde, Djibouti, Equatorial Guinea, Eritrea, Guinea-Bissau, São Tomé and Príncipe, Seychelles, Sudan and Western Sahara), the cells rest on the relation estimated in other countries (surveyed_country is false). For 2,066 cells on coasts and lake shores whose center lies outside every administrative polygon, we use the nearest region of the same country (nearest_unit is true). For 1,084 cells, the population comes from GHS-POP (population_gapfilled is true). The columns model_sd_* give the standard deviation of the five learners’ predictions for a cell and rank cells by how much the learners disagree.
